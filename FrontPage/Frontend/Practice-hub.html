Free IELTS Practice by Question Type: Reading, Listening and Writing

A full IELTS mock test shows your overall level, but practising by question type is what raises your band score. IELTS Mockpoint lets you train each question type separately, in the same interface you will meet on test day, with Cambridge-style timing and format. It is free IELTS practice with no sign-up.

IELTS Reading question types

Practise note completion, True/False/Not Given, matching headings, multiple choice, summary completion, matching information, sentence completion, sentence endings and matching people. Reading gives 60 minutes for 3 passages and 40 questions, so aim for about 20 minutes each. Try our Cambridge IELTS Reading practice for full passages.

IELTS Listening question types

Train form, note, table and sentence completion, multiple choice, matching, map labelling, plan and diagram labelling and short answer questions. You hear the audio once, so predicting answers, following signpost words and accurate spelling all matter. Continue with Cambridge IELTS Listening practice.

IELTS Writing task types

Cover Task 1 line graphs, bar charts, pie charts, tables, processes, maps and letters, plus every Task 2 essay type: opinion, discussion, problem and solution, advantages and disadvantages, and two-part questions. See Cambridge IELTS Writing practice and our writing samples.

Practise question types, then take a free IELTS mock test

Use question-type practice for three to four days, review every error, then take a free IELTS mock test to check timing and stamina. Repeat the loop and track how each skill improves with our band score calculator.

IELTS practice questions, answered

Is IELTS practice by question type free on IELTS Mockpoint?

Yes. Reading, Listening and Writing practice by question type is free and needs no Student ID or passcode. You can also take a free IELTS mock test.

Which IELTS question types can I practise?

Reading: note completion, True/False/Not Given, matching headings, multiple choice, summary completion, matching information, sentence completion, sentence endings and matching people. Listening: form, note, table and sentence completion, multiple choice, matching, map and plan labelling, and short answer. Writing: Task 1 charts, maps, processes and letters, and every Task 2 essay type.

Should I practise by question type or take full IELTS mock tests?

Do both. Practise the question types that cost you marks, review every mistake, then take a full timed mock test to check stamina and timing.

Is this Cambridge IELTS practice?

Our practice follows the Cambridge IELTS format and timing, so you can train in the same style as the official books. IELTS Mockpoint is not affiliated with Cambridge, the British Council or IDP.

How long should I spend on Writing Task 1 and Task 2?

About 20 minutes for Task 1 (at least 150 words) and 40 minutes for Task 2 (at least 250 words).

How many hours a week should I practise for IELTS?

Most candidates see steady progress with 8 to 10 hours a week across all four skills, plus a full mock every one or two weeks.
