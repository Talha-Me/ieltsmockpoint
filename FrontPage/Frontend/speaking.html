IELTS Speaking test format

How the IELTS Speaking exam works

The Speaking test is a one-to-one conversation with a certified examiner. It is recorded, lasts 11–14 minutes, and is the same for Academic and General Training. Choose a part to see what happens.

4–5 MINUTES

Part 1: Introduction and interview

The examiner checks your ID, then asks short questions about familiar topics.

What to do

  • Answer in 2–3 sentences, not one word
  • Add a reason or a short example
  • Stay relaxed and natural

Common topics

Topics: home, work or study, hometown, hobbies, food, daily routine, transport, technology.Example question: “Do you prefer to spend your free time indoors or outdoors? Why?”Tip: use Answer + Reason + Example.
3–4 MINUTES

Part 2: Individual long turn

You get a task card, 1 minute to prepare with paper and pencil, then speak for 1–2 minutes.

What to do

  • Use the bullet points on the card as a structure
  • Keep speaking until the examiner stops you
  • Expect 1–2 short follow-up questions

Task card example

Describe a place you visited that you enjoyed.You should say: where it was, when you went there, what you did there, and explain why you enjoyed it.Tip: tell it as a short story: past, present, feeling.
4–5 MINUTES

Part 3: Two-way discussion

The examiner asks deeper, more abstract questions linked to the Part 2 topic.

What to do

  • Give opinions and explain them
  • Compare past and present, or people and societies
  • Speculate: “It might…”, “I’d say…”

Example questions

Example: “How has tourism changed the way people travel over the last 20 years?”Example: “Do you think governments should protect popular tourist places?”Tip: longer, well-reasoned answers score higher.
IELTS Speaking marking

How IELTS Speaking is marked

The examiner scores you on four criteria, each worth 25% of your Speaking band. The four scores are averaged and rounded to the nearest half or whole band.

25%

Fluency and Coherence

Speaking at a natural pace without long pauses, and linking ideas logically with connectors.

25%

Lexical Resource

Range and accuracy of vocabulary, including idioms and paraphrasing when you forget a word.

25%

Grammatical Range and Accuracy

A mix of simple and complex sentences, correct tenses and few errors that block meaning.

25%

Pronunciation

Clear sounds, word stress, rhythm and intonation that are easy to follow.

How to practise IELTS Speaking

Practise Speaking at home in three steps

You do not need a partner. Use your phone and a simple routine.

1

Record yourself

Answer Part 1, 2 and 3 style questions with a voice recorder. Time your Part 2 talk to 2 minutes.

2

Listen and score

Play it back and rate yourself on the four criteria. Note pauses, repeated words and grammar slips.

3

Repeat and upgrade

Record again with better vocabulary and clearer structure. Small changes add up to a higher band.

Extend your answers

Do not stop at yes or no. Give a reason and an example every time.

Don't memorise scripts

Examiners notice rehearsed answers. Practise ideas and phrases, not full paragraphs.

Paraphrase when stuck

If you forget a word, explain it in other words. It shows vocabulary range.

Stress and intonation

Clear stress and a natural tone matter more than a perfect accent.

IELTS Speaking FAQ

Your IELTS Speaking questions, answered

How long is the IELTS Speaking test?

It lasts 11–14 minutes: Part 1 takes 4–5 minutes, Part 2 takes 3–4 minutes including preparation, and Part 3 takes 4–5 minutes.

How is IELTS Speaking marked?

An examiner scores Fluency and Coherence, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range and Accuracy, and Pronunciation equally. The average is rounded to the nearest half or whole band.

Is the Speaking test recorded?

Yes. The test is audio recorded so the result can be reviewed if needed.

Is Speaking different for Academic and General Training?

No. Both use the same format and the same marking criteria.

Can I take notes in Part 2?

Yes. You have 1 minute to prepare, with paper and a pencil, before you speak for 1–2 minutes.

Is the Speaking mock test on IELTS Mockpoint free?

No. It is coming soon and will be paid. Listening, Reading and Writing practice remain free.

Can I practise IELTS Speaking without a partner?

Yes. Record yourself, listen back, score yourself on the four criteria and repeat. Regular short sessions work best.

What if I do not understand a question?

Ask the examiner to repeat it politely. It will not lower your score if you do not overuse it.
