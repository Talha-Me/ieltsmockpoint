The IELTS Listening test checks how well you understand spoken English in everyday, educational and academic situations. It has 4 parts and 40 questions, and the recording is played only once. IELTS Listening Question Types and Tips for a Higher Band Score

Knowing each question type and how the four parts work is the fastest way to improve your IELTS listening score. Use the free mock tests above to apply these strategies under real exam conditions.

IELTS Listening Part 1: everyday conversation

Part 1 is a conversation between two people in a daily social situation, such as booking a service or making an enquiry. Answers are usually names, numbers, dates, prices and addresses, so practise spelling names and writing numbers quickly and accurately. Listen for corrections, because speakers often change a detail after giving it.

IELTS Listening Part 2: a monologue in a social context

Part 2 is a talk by one speaker about a local event, facility or tour. Map and plan labelling is common here, so learn directions and location words such as "opposite", "next to", "behind" and "along the corridor".

IELTS Listening Part 3: academic discussion

Part 3 is a conversation between up to four people, often students and a tutor, discussing an assignment or research. Questions test opinions and attitudes, so the speakers often mention several options before choosing one. Pay attention to words that signal agreement, disagreement or a change of mind.

IELTS Listening Part 4: university lecture

Part 4 is a monologue on an academic topic with no break between questions. Because the lecture is fast and dense, follow the structure and the signposting words such as "firstly", "however" and "as a result", and use the questions to predict what the speaker will say next.

Listening tips that work in every part

  • Use the preview time. Read the questions, underline keywords and predict the type of answer (a number, a name, an adjective).
  • Follow the order. Answers appear in the same order as the questions, except in matching tasks.
  • Check the word limit. An answer with too many words loses the mark, so follow the instruction exactly.
  • Watch your spelling and grammar. Spelling must be correct, and singular and plural forms matter. UK and US spellings are both accepted.
  • Beware of distractors. Speakers often mention a wrong answer first, then correct it.
  • Never get stuck. If you miss an answer, move on at once so you do not miss the next one, and make a guess later. There is no negative marking.
  • Leave nothing blank. Always write an answer, even if you are unsure.

IELTS Listening Practice Test Guide: Scoring, Accents and Study Plan

If you are searching for an IELTS listening test, a listening mock test free or the best way to practise IELTS listening online, this guide explains how the test works and how to use each free mock on this page to raise your band score.

How IELTS Listening is scored

Each correct answer is worth one mark, and there is no negative marking, so always answer every question. Your raw score out of 40 is converted into a band from 0 to 9. After each mock test you see which answers were right or wrong, so you can study your mistakes and find patterns in the question types you miss.

Time to check your answers

In the paper-based test you get extra time at the end to transfer your answers to the answer sheet, and in the computer-delivered test you get a short review period. Either way, you listen to each recording only once, so practise writing correct answers as you listen rather than relying on the review time.

Get used to different English accents

The IELTS Listening test uses a range of native-speaker accents, including British, Australian, New Zealand, Canadian and North American. Regular practice with Cambridge recordings is the best way to become comfortable with each of them. Listening to podcasts, news and lectures in different accents will strengthen the same skills.

Common mistakes in the IELTS listening test

  • Missing the next answer while thinking about the previous one.
  • Writing the first answer you hear and ignoring the speaker's correction.
  • Spelling mistakes in names, places and common words.
  • Exceeding the word limit or forgetting the plural form.
  • Not using the preview time to read and predict the answers.
  • Losing focus in Part 4 after a long period of listening.

A simple 4-week IELTS Listening practice plan

  • Week 1: take one full test to find your baseline, then study every mistake and list your weak parts and question types.
  • Week 2: use part-by-part practice daily, focusing on the two question types that cost you the most marks.
  • Week 3: sit two full mock tests and practise listening only once, with no pausing and no replaying.
  • Week 4: repeat weak tests, work on spelling and note-taking, and take a final full mock in exam conditions.

Frequently asked questions about the free IELTS Listening mock test

Quick answers about our free IELTS listening mock tests and Cambridge IELTS listening practice.

Is this IELTS Listening mock test really free?

Yes. You can practise Cambridge IELTS Listening tests online on IELTS Mockpoint for free and get your score straight after you finish.

How long is the IELTS Listening test?

The IELTS Listening test takes about 30 minutes and has 40 questions across 4 parts. Each recording is played only once, so you need to answer as you listen.

How many parts and questions are in IELTS Listening?

There are 4 parts with 10 questions each, so 40 questions in total. Part 1 is questions 1 to 10, Part 2 is 11 to 20, Part 3 is 21 to 30 and Part 4 is 31 to 40.

Can I practise one part at a time?

Yes. Every test has a Practice mode where you choose Part 1, Part 2, Part 3 or Part 4 and work on your weak areas without sitting the full test.

Which Cambridge IELTS books are available?

Cambridge IELTS 12 to 21 are available, each with 4 full listening tests, which gives you 40 free IELTS listening mock tests in total.

How is my IELTS Listening band score calculated?

Each correct answer earns one mark out of 40. Your raw score is then converted to a band from 0 to 9. The same Listening test and conversion are used for Academic and General Training.

What question types appear in IELTS Listening?

Common types include form, note, table and flow-chart completion, multiple choice, matching, map and plan labelling, sentence completion and short-answer questions.

Can I listen to the IELTS Listening audio twice?

No. In the real exam each recording is played only once. For the best results, practise with a single listening so your skills match test-day conditions.

How many correct answers do I need for Band 6, 7 or 8 in IELTS Listening?

As a rough guide, about 23 out of 40 gives Band 6, about 30 out of 40 gives Band 7 and about 35 out of 40 gives Band 8. Conversion tables can vary slightly between test versions.

Which accents are used in the IELTS Listening test?

The test uses a range of native-speaker accents, including British, Australian, New Zealand, Canadian and North American. Practising with Cambridge recordings helps you get used to all of them.

Do spelling and grammar matter in IELTS Listening answers?

Yes. Spelling must be correct to earn a mark, and singular and plural forms matter. Both UK and US spellings are accepted, and you can write answers in capital or lower-case letters.

Is there negative marking in IELTS Listening?

No. Wrong answers do not lose marks, so you should never leave a question blank. Make an educated guess if you are unsure.

How often should I take an IELTS listening mock test?

Most learners improve fastest by taking one full timed mock test per week and doing part-by-part practice on other days. Always review every wrong answer before you move to the next test.

How can I improve my IELTS Listening score quickly?

Use the preview time to predict answers, follow the order of the questions, check the word limit, watch your spelling and listen for corrections. Repeating weak question types in part-by-part practice usually gives the quickest gains.

Are Cambridge IELTS 20 and 21 listening tests available?

Yes. Cambridge IELTS 12 to 21 are available, each with 4 full listening tests, so you can practise the newest Cambridge IELTS listening tests together with older books.
