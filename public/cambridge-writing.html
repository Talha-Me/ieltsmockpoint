What is the IELTS Writing test?

The IELTS Writing test lasts 60 minutes and has 2 tasks. In IELTS Academic Writing, Task 1 asks you to describe a graph, chart, table, process or map in at least 150 words, and Task 2 asks you to write an essay of at least 250 words in response to a point of view, argument or problem. Task 2 carries twice as much weight as Task 1 in your final Writing band.

IELTS Writing Task 1 and Task 2: Structure, Tips and Common Mistakes

Knowing exactly what examiners look for is the fastest way to improve your IELTS writing score. Use the free tests above to apply these strategies under real exam conditions.

How to structure IELTS Writing Task 1

  • Introduction: paraphrase the question in one or two sentences.
  • Overview: state the main trends, biggest differences or key stages without detailed numbers. A clear overview is essential for a high score.
  • Body paragraph 1 and 2: group related data together, select key figures and make accurate comparisons.
  • Language: use a formal, objective tone, and do not give opinions or explain reasons that the data does not show.

How to structure IELTS Writing Task 2

  • Introduction: paraphrase the topic and give a clear answer or thesis.
  • Body paragraph 1 and 2: start each paragraph with one main idea, then explain it and support it with a reason or example.
  • Conclusion: summarise your main points and restate your position without adding new ideas.
  • Answer every part: if the question has two parts, make sure both are clearly covered.

The four IELTS Writing assessment criteria

Your writing is marked on four equally weighted criteria: Task Achievement (Task 1) or Task Response (Task 2), Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource and Grammatical Range and Accuracy. Each task receives a band score, and Task 2 counts twice as much as Task 1 in the final Writing band. There is no raw score conversion table for Writing, because trained examiners assess your answer against the band descriptors.

Time management for the 60-minute Writing test

  • Spend about 20 minutes on Task 1 and about 40 minutes on Task 2.
  • Plan for 2 to 3 minutes before you start writing each task.
  • Leave 2 minutes at the end to proofread for spelling, grammar and word count.
  • Do not spend extra time perfecting Task 1 at the cost of Task 2, because Task 2 carries more marks.

Common mistakes in IELTS Writing

  • Writing fewer than 150 words in Task 1 or fewer than 250 words in Task 2, which lowers your score.
  • Missing the overview in Task 1 or describing every number instead of selecting key features.
  • Not answering all parts of the Task 2 question or giving an unclear position.
  • Memorised, off-topic paragraphs that do not match the question.
  • Repeating the same words and sentence patterns instead of using a range of vocabulary and structures.
  • Weak paragraphing and overuse of linking words such as "firstly" and "moreover".

A simple 4-week IELTS Writing practice plan

  • Week 1: take one full test to find your baseline, then compare your answers with the four criteria and note your weakest areas.
  • Week 2: practise Task 1 types in turn (graphs, tables, processes, maps) and focus on writing a clear overview.
  • Week 3: practise each Task 2 essay type, and spend 5 minutes planning every essay before you write.
  • Week 4: sit two full timed tests, review your answers, and polish your grammar, vocabulary and proofreading.

IELTS Academic Writing vs General Training Writing

Both versions have 2 tasks in 60 minutes, and Task 2 is an essay in both. The difference is Task 1: Academic candidates describe visual information, while General Training candidates write a letter. The Cambridge tests on this page are Academic Writing, and the essay skills you build in Task 2 also help General Training candidates.

Frequently asked questions about IELTS Writing practice

How long is the IELTS Writing test?

The IELTS Writing test lasts 60 minutes: about 20 minutes for Task 1 (minimum 150 words) and 40 minutes for Task 2 (minimum 250 words).

Which task carries more marks?

Task 2 carries twice as much weight as Task 1 in your Writing band score, so spend the extra time on it.

Can I practise only Task 1 or only Task 2?

Yes. Every test card lets you take the full test or practise Task 1 or Task 2 on its own. Use the Full Test / Task 1 / Task 2 switch above, or the small buttons on each card.

What are the IELTS Writing assessment criteria?

Your writing is scored on Task Achievement/Response, Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range and Accuracy.

Is this IELTS Writing practice test free?

Yes, Cambridge Writing practice on IELTS Mockpoint is free. Create an account to save your progress.

Which Cambridge IELTS books are available for Writing practice?

Cambridge IELTS 12 to 18 are available, each with 4 full writing tests. That gives you 28 free IELTS writing practice tests and 56 tasks in total.

How many words should I write in IELTS Writing Task 1 and Task 2?

Write at least 150 words for Task 1 and at least 250 words for Task 2. Answers that are shorter than the minimum are penalised, so always check your word count before you finish.

What types of questions appear in IELTS Academic Writing Task 1?

Task 1 shows visual information such as line graphs, bar charts, pie charts, tables, process diagrams, maps or a combination of charts. You describe and compare the key features in your own words.

What types of essays appear in IELTS Writing Task 2?

Common Task 2 essay types are opinion (agree or disagree), discussion of both views, problem and solution, advantages and disadvantages, and two-part questions.

What is the difference between IELTS Academic and General Training Writing?

Both versions have 2 tasks in 60 minutes and Task 2 is an essay in both. In Academic Writing, Task 1 describes visual data, while in General Training Task 1 is a letter.

How is IELTS Writing scored?

Trained examiners give each task a band score using four equally weighted criteria: Task Achievement or Response, Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range and Accuracy. Task 2 counts twice as much as Task 1 in the final Writing band.

How can I improve my IELTS Writing score quickly?

Practise under real timing, always write a clear overview in Task 1, answer every part of the Task 2 question with a clear position, support each idea with reasons and examples, and leave time to proofread. Reviewing your answers against the four criteria after every test gives the fastest gains.

How often should I practise IELTS Writing?

Many learners improve steadily with 3 to 4 timed writing tasks per week and one full 60-minute test each week. Always review your answers carefully before you move on to the next test.

Is this the same as the real computer-delivered IELTS Writing test?

The tests are built to feel like the on-screen exam, so you can practise typing your answers, managing the 60-minute timer and moving between Task 1 and Task 2 in a familiar interface.
